# Round 9t group E1: extrude drag interactions and reference plane options

Status: built. Design written before code, updated to match what was built.

## a. DECISIONS entries

D525: Extrude drag interactions: Alt drag snap and drag a sketch without the panel (TOOL_SPEC T5.2 item 4, T5.4). (1) Alt snap: while an extrude depth arrow (`depth` of Extrude or Extruded Cut with Blind or Mid Plane, and `depth2`) is dragged with Alt held, the 3D view looks under the cursor with its normal detection (the AIS context `MoveTo`, on the UI thread, picking only, no kernel builds) for a vertex or face of the shown part. A vertex wins over a face and gives its point. A flat face parallel to the sketch plane gives its plane, so the depth is exactly its distance; any other face gives the point under the cursor. The new travel is the distance from the arrow's start along its direction to that point; on Mid Plane the total depth is twice it, so the end lands on the point. Behind the start a Direction 1 arrow that flips (D147) flips; other arrows stop at their smallest value. A small marker (a ringed plus in the accent colour) sits on the snapped point and the value beside the arrow gets "to vertex" or "to face". Without Alt, or over nothing, the drag works as before (ruler and free value). The arrows that snap carry a new `PreviewHandle.snaps` flag, set only by Extrude, so the Offset from Face arrow, revolve arrows, move face and every other handle are unchanged. Alt and left click (select the next item) still works: it happens only on a click without a drag on the part, while a press on an arrow starts a handle drag and never selects, so the two never clash. (2) Drag a sketch: with nothing open for editing (no feature panel, sketch or reference panel), a plain left click (no Ctrl, Shift, Alt or Meta) in the 3D view casts the view ray onto the plane of every finished sketch shown there. Hits behind the part point under the cursor (more than 0.01 mm) are left out, so a sketch hidden by the part is not clicked. A worker finds the first hit that lies inside one of that sketch's regions (D193 regions, the cached `SketchOutput.regions()`) and probes the part 0.01 mm on each side of the plane with the solid classifier. Material on one side makes it start as a cut going into the part; material on neither side makes it a boss along the sketch normal (and a boss always goes away from material). The new feature is an ordinary Extrude: Blind, `refs.regions` holding the clicked region, the default name ("Extrude 1" or "Cut 1"), and a first depth of one ruler step (D146) of the region's size. The normal feature preview shows it with its arrow, ruler, flip past the start and Alt snap; dragging sets the depth. After release a small bar near the cursor offers Boss, Cut, OK, Cancel and Edit in Panel. Boss and Cut swap the operation, the name and the direction (into or away from the part). OK (or Enter) adds the feature with the normal undoable Add Feature command and selects it in the tree. Edit in Panel opens the full Extrude panel on the same feature, nothing added until its OK. Escape (in the view or on the bar), Cancel, a click elsewhere, a changed document, opening a panel or sketch, or a tab change drop the arrow and preview without changing the document; the feature id taken for it is given back. Nothing new is stored in files.
Status: Default
Reason: Both reuse the existing drag handles, preview, regions and new feature command, so the result is an ordinary feature and nothing new is saved.

D526: Reference plane Project and Parallel to screen (PHASE2_SPEC P2.10 item 1). (1) Method `project`: first pick a point (vertex, sketch point or reference point), second pick a face (any face; a curved face is the usual case). By default the point goes to the nearest point of the face (shortest distance), and the plane passes through it, normal to the face there, pointing out of the part. With `along_normal` (Along Sketch Normal tick box, only for a sketch point) the point moves along its sketch's normal, either way, to the nearest place where that line meets the face. Errors: "Plane 1: only a sketch point can be projected along the sketch normal. Pick a sketch point or turn off Along Sketch Normal." and "Plane 1: the point does not reach the face along the sketch normal. Turn off Along Sketch Normal or pick another face." A point picked with a face, cylinder or cone face (in either order) is guessed as `project`; a point and a flat face stay Parallel through a point. (2) Method `screen` (Parallel to screen): one pick, a point. The plane passes through it, normal to the view direction, pointing toward the viewer. The direction is taken from the 3D view when the method is chosen and stored as the plain numbers `normal_x`, `normal_y` and `normal_z` (a unit vector, rounded to 12 places), so rebuilds never depend on the camera; core reads only the stored vector. A Use Current View button, shown only for this method, takes the view direction again. Without a stored direction the panel says "Press Use Current View to take the view direction." and the feature error says "Plane 1: the view direction is missing. Press Use Current View to take it." Both methods take `flip_normal`. The keys are optional and written only for these methods, so older files are unchanged (FDEF_SPEC 5.3.20). (3) A sketch point pick now also carries its sketch normal (`Geo.normal`); no other method reads it.
Status: Default
Reason: These were the last two plane constraints in P2.10; storing the captured normal keeps rebuilds stable and keeps the camera out of core.

## b. CHANGELOG "Added" bullets

- Hold Alt while dragging an extrude depth arrow over a part face or vertex to snap the end to it. Click inside a region of a finished sketch and drag its arrow to make a boss or cut without the panel; a small bar then switches Boss or Cut, adds it, cancels or opens the full panel (D525).
- Reference planes: Project (through a point projected onto a face, normal to the face there, nearest point or along the sketch normal) and Parallel to Screen through a point (D526).

## c. User guide text

Page `docs/user_guide/boss-and-cut.md` (Extrude section, after the drag arrows):

- Hold Alt while you drag a depth arrow. Move over a vertex or a face of the part and the end snaps to it. A marker shows the point, and the value says "to vertex" or "to face". A flat face parallel to the sketch gives its exact distance.
- You can extrude without opening the panel. With no tool open, click inside a closed region of a finished sketch. An arrow appears on it. A region on the part starts as a cut into the part; a region off the part starts as a boss.
- Drag the arrow to set the depth. When you let go, a small bar appears. Choose Boss or Cut, then OK to add the feature, or Cancel. Edit in Panel opens the full Extrude panel. Escape or a click elsewhere cancels.

Page `docs/user_guide/reference-geometry.md`, plane methods:

- Project onto a face: pick a point and a face. The plane goes through the point moved onto the face and is normal to the face there. For a sketch point, tick Along Sketch Normal to move it along the sketch normal instead of to the nearest point.
- Parallel to screen: pick a point. The plane goes through it and faces you. The view direction is kept when you choose the method, so turning the view later does not move the plane. Press Use Current View to take the view again.

## d. Left out

1. T5.4: the bar has no depth field; the depth is set by dragging (with ruler, Alt snap and flip) or in the full panel through Edit in Panel. Clicking the value beside the arrow does not open the value box here, since that box edits a panel field.
2. T5.4: regions of finished sketches are not highlighted on hover before the click. Showing region faces for every shown sketch would build faces for all of them on each rebuild; the click finds the region from the sketch's cached regions instead.
3. T5.4: only one region per drag, and only Extrude (no Revolve, which needs an axis the drag cannot give).
4. T5.2 item 4: Alt snap reads the shown part only (not reference planes, sketch points or other sketches) and is not offered on revolve angle arrows, which the spec does not ask for.
5. The 3D view parts (Alt snap picking, the snap marker, the click ray) need a GPU and are tested through their pure helpers and the controller, as the other drag handle tests do.
