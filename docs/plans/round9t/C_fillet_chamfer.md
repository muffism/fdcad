# Round 9t group C: fillet and chamfer options (D518 to D520)

Status: built. The design was written first (commit dcafcf5) and this note now matches what was built.

## a. Decisions

D518: Face fillet and full round fillet (PHASE2_SPEC P2.4 item 1). (1) Two new `fillet_type` values, `"face"` and `"full_round"`. Old files keep `"constant"` and `"variable"` and open and save unchanged. (2) Face fillet: two face sets in `refs.faces1` and `refs.faces2` and the `radius`. Every edge shared by a face of set 1 and a face of set 2 is rounded with the radius by the same kernel fillet as a constant fillet, so new faces are named `fillet/<edge name>` and failing edges are pinpointed (D213 item 4) with the "Reduce the radius" fix only ("Fillet the rest" is not offered, as the edges are not picks). Sets that share no edge fail with "Fillet 3: the two face sets do not meet at an edge. Pick faces that touch each other." (3) Full round: three face sets, `refs.faces1` (side 1), `refs.centre` (centre faces) and `refs.faces2` (side 2). Each set must be flat faces on one plane, and the two side planes must be parallel and apart. The centre faces are replaced by a round of radius half the distance between the sides, tangent to both sides and touching the centre plane (the centre face may slope across the rib). It is built exactly: a prism whose cross section is the region between the two side planes above the round's arc is cut away. The prism runs along the round's axis (the side normal crossed with the centre normal) over the length of the centre faces. The round face is named `full_round/<first centre face name>`. The radius is computed, so the panel hides the radius; the stored `radius` is written as before but not used. A rib of width w and length L loses exactly L (w w / 2 less pi w w / 8). (4) Full round errors, each with what to do: a set is empty; a set is not flat or not on one plane; the sides are not parallel ("a full round needs two parallel side faces") or on one plane; the centre face is parallel to the sides; the centre faces do not lie between the sides; a side ends before the round's axis ("the first side is shorter than half the width (2 mm), so the round does not fit"); the cut does not give one valid body. (5) Panel: a Face Sets group with one pick list per set and a "Pick into" list; after each pick the next set is chosen, so one click per set is enough, and clicking a list makes it the target. Face sets are named Face Set 1 and 2, or Side Face Set 1, Centre Face Set and Side Face Set 2 for the full round. Items to Fillet is hidden for these types, and the whole Fillet Parameters group for the full round. (6) Left out: face sets that do not meet (for example two walls with a gap). The kernel's fillet maker only rounds existing edges, and a rolling ball blend between faces that do not touch needs a surface builder the kernel does not offer reliably.
Status: Default
Reason: Rounding the shared edges reuses the tested fillet path, and the prism cut gives an exact, valid single solid for the full round.

D519: Fillet assistant, partial edges, cross sections and setback (P2.4 items 1, 3 and 6). (1) Fillet assistant: in Items to Fillet, a scope list (Whole Part or an earlier solid feature) and two buttons, Add Convex Edges and Add Concave Edges. Each click adds one item to the list ("Concave edges of the part", "Convex edges of Extrude 2"), stored in `refs.auto_edges` as `{"convexity": "convex"}` or `{"convexity": "concave", "feature": "f6"}`. The edges are found when the feature rebuilds with the kernel's edge analysis (smooth edges and edges of one face are skipped), so the item follows later changes to the part and no kernel work runs on the UI thread. A scope feature with no faces left fails naming it. Works with Constant Size and Variable Size (these edges use the main radius). (2) Partial edges (Constant Size only): a checkable Partial Edges group with Distance or Percent, a start and an end, and Flip. Keys: `partial` (`"distance"` or `"percent"`), `start_distance` and `end_distance` (lengths) or `start_percent` and `end_percent` (numbers), and `partial_flip`. The range is measured along each item edge from its first end (the other end with Flip). The kernel cannot round part of an edge (a radius law may not reach zero), so each edge is done in steps: the part is split by the planes square to the edge at the start and the end, the piece of the edge in the middle piece is rounded, and the pieces are fused and their split faces merged again. The round ends square at the start and end planes. Faces that were only split get their names back; the new face is `fillet/<edge name>`. A range that is empty, reversed or longer than the edge fails with "the partial range on edge X must start before it ends and stay on the edge (0 to 50 mm long)". A kernel failure names the edge and suggests a smaller radius or moving the ends away from corners. Partial edges need a part with one body. The fillet is done edge by edge, so several partial edges cost several kernel steps. (3) Left out: start and end "by reference" (a picked point or plane), because distance and percent cover the common cases and a reference needs its own pick list. (4) Cross sections, left out: the kernel's fillet maker offers three section shapes (rational, quasi angular and polynomial), but all three are ways to build a circular arc. None makes a conic or a curvature continuous section. (5) Setback at a corner vertex, left out: the kernel builds corner blends itself and has no setting for how far they are set back.
Status: Default
Reason: Stored convexity items keep the rounding in step with the part, and splitting the part gives exact partial rounds with the kernel's own fillet.

D520: Offset face chamfer and face to face chamfer (P2.5 item 1). (1) Two new `chamfer_type` values, `"offset_face"` and `"face_face"`. (2) Offset face: picked edges and faces as for Distance Distance, `method` symmetric or asymmetric, `distance` and `distance2`, and `flip`. Each distance is an offset measured square to a face: `distance` from the reference face (D216 item 2), `distance2` from the other face. The chamfer runs along the lines where each face, moved inward by its offset, meets the other face. With phi the angle between the faces across the chamfered corner (pi less the angle between the outward normals), the distance along the reference face is `distance2 / sin(phi)` and along the other face `distance / sin(phi)`; these feed the kernel's two distance chamfer. At 90 degrees this equals Distance Distance with the two distances on swapped faces. The angle must be the same along the whole edge (checked at both ends and the middle), so straight edges between flat faces and round edges of holes and bosses work; others fail with "the angle between the faces changes along edge X. Use Distance Distance for this edge." Faces that meet smoothly fail too. (3) Face to face: two face sets, `refs.faces1` and `refs.faces2`; the edges where they meet are chamfered. `method`: `"symmetric"` (`distance` along both faces), `"asymmetric"` (`distance` along the set 1 face, `distance2` along the set 2 face) or `"width"`, with the new optional `width` length: the width of the chamfer face, so the distance along each face is `width / (2 sin(phi / 2))`. The `distance` key is still written with Width (hidden in the panel) so files keep the required key. Flip is hidden (set 1 decides). Sets that share no edge fail like the face fillet. Failing edges are pinpointed with the "Reduce the distance" or "Reduce the width" fix ("Chamfer the rest" is not offered). (4) The method list shows Width only for Face to Face (the item is disabled for other types and the choice falls back to Symmetric). New faces are named `chamfer/<edge name>` as before. (5) Left out: face sets that do not meet, for the reason in D518 item 6.
Status: Default
Reason: Turning the offsets and the width into along face distances reuses the kernel's exact two distance chamfer.

## b. CHANGELOG (Added)

- Fillet: Face and Full Round types, a fillet assistant that adds all convex or all concave edges of the part or of one feature, and partial edge fillets by distance or percent (D518, D519).
- Chamfer: Offset Face and Face to Face types, with a chamfer width option for Face to Face (D520).

## c. User guide

Page: `docs/user_guide/modify.md`, in the Fillet and Chamfer sections.

Fillet:
- Face: pick faces for Face Set 1 and Face Set 2. The edges where the two sets meet are rounded with the radius.
- Full Round: click a side face, the centre face and the other side face. The centre face is replaced by a half round that touches both sides. The sides must be flat and parallel, and the radius is half the distance between them.
- Fillet assistant: in Items to Fillet, choose Whole Part or a feature, then click Add Convex Edges or Add Concave Edges. The list keeps one item that finds the edges again when the part changes.
- Partial Edges (Constant Size): tick the group, choose Distance or Percent and type a start and an end. Only that part of each edge is rounded. Flip measures from the other end of the edge.

Chamfer:
- Offset Face: the distances are measured square to each face, as if each face moved inward by its distance.
- Face to Face: pick two face sets. The edges where they meet are chamfered by one distance, two distances (the first along Face Set 1), or the width of the chamfer face.

## d. Left out

- Face fillet and face to face chamfer between face sets that do not touch (D518 item 6, D520 item 5): the kernel only rounds or bevels existing edges.
- Partial edge start and end "by reference" (D519 item 3).
- Conic and curvature continuous fillet cross sections (D519 item 4): the kernel's section shapes are all circular.
- Setback at a corner vertex (D519 item 5): the kernel has no setting for it.
