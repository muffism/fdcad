66. 2026-11-13: Local booleans. On the bike frame 115 kernel booleans take 30 of the 42 s of a first open, about 0.5 s each, because each one works on the whole frame of lofted tubes (oriented boxes and no fuzzy value made no difference, D1090). Idea: cut or join only the solid near the tool (a box round it) and stitch the result back, keeping face names. Likely 3 to 5 times faster on large parts; needs care with naming and many tests. Researched 2026-11-15 on the machine bed (5406 faces, its top face has 955 holes), after the owner asked for all features to load faster: (a) the kernel spends 0.13 s finding where the tool meets the body and 0.46 s building the whole result again, however small the tool; (b) a prototype splits only the faces near the tool with the kernel's splitter, sorts the pieces inside or outside by counting the faces a ray crosses (face boxes in numpy), and splices them into a copy of the body's shell; (c) a face with many holes is stood in for by a small patch of its plane round the tool, carrying the holes that fall inside the patch, and the new loops go back into the big face, so a new hole no longer rebuilds the 955 old ones. Six cuts and joins (through hole, blind hole, pocket flush with the top, hole beside a hole, boss on the top, boss half sunk) gave the kernel's volume to 1e-8 mm3 and its face count, valid, in 0.14 to 0.32 s against 1.9 to 3.0 s.
